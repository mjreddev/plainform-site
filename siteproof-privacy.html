SiteProof Privacy Policy

SiteProof · MJRed Dev LLC (trading as Plainform) · Effective October 7, 2026

Plainform apps policies: CallRules File Zone SiteProof
Contents: Part 1. Using SiteProof on your own (no account) Part 2. SiteProof Team (optional company account) Deleting your account and your data For everyone

SiteProof is a camera and documentation app for job sites. You can use it in two ways:

  • On your own, without an account. Everything stays on your phone. SiteProof has no servers involved, and SiteProof itself collects nothing. This is how the app works when you install it.
  • With SiteProof Team, an optional company account. A company signs in so its crew can share projects through the cloud, and the office can use the SiteProof website and share work with its clients. Team data is stored on servers we run with our service providers, in the United States.

Each way is described in its own part below. One optional feature, translation (Pro), uses a Google component that talks to Google in both cases; it has its own section.

The short version.

  • Without an account, SiteProof collects nothing. No account, no analytics, no advertising, no crash-reporting service. Your photos stay on your phone and leave it only when you choose to send them.
  • SiteProof Team is optional and only for companies. If your company uses it, the company's projects, photos and notes are stored in the cloud so the crew and the office can work on them together. The company owns that data, and its owner controls it.
  • Clients see only what a company shares with them, through a private link or by signing in with their email address.
  • You can delete your Team account at any time, in the app or on the website. It is deleted at once. A company's owner can delete the whole company; it is switched off at once and deleted for good after 30 days.
  • We do not sell your data, and we do not use it for advertising. We use service providers (Supabase, Cloudflare, Resend and Google, including Firebase Cloud Messaging for Team phone notifications, and Stripe for Team billing when it launches) only to run SiteProof.
  • Translation (Pro) is optional. If you use it, Google ML Kit sends Google diagnostic and usage data. The text you translate stays on your phone.

Part 1. Using SiteProof on your own (no account)

What the app stores, and where

Everything below is stored only in SiteProof's private storage on your phone.

  • Photos you take or import, with their time and time zone, GPS location and accuracy, and street address.
  • What you add: names, notes, tags, markup, checklists, and project and job details, including client contact details you type in.
  • Company details you enter for report covers, including a logo.
  • Settings.

Uninstalling SiteProof deletes all of it, unless you have set up a backup or exported your library.

Without an account, none of this is sent to us. We have no copy of it.

When your data leaves the phone — only when you choose

  • Reports and exports (PDF, ZIP + CSV) go wherever you share or save them. Blurred or redacted areas are burned into every image in a report or export; the unredacted original is not included.
  • Backup folder. If you choose one, SiteProof copies your photos (including unredacted originals), their details and your reports into that folder. If the folder is synced by a service such as Google Drive or Dropbox, that service's own terms apply. The library export file holds the same information.
  • Share to computer (Pro). When you turn it on, the phone serves a web page to computers on the same Wi-Fi network only, protected by a pairing code shown on the phone.
    • Nothing goes through the internet or any server.
    • It turns itself off after 30 minutes without use, when you leave the Wi-Fi, and after 6 hours at most.
    • Redacted photos' originals are never served.

Your choices

  • You can deny any permission. The app keeps working with what you allow.
  • You can delete any photo, job or project in the app.
  • You can remove all data by uninstalling the app, or by clearing its storage in Android's settings.
  • You can stop using translation at any time and delete the language packs in Settings › Translation.

Without an account we hold no data about you, so there is nothing to request from us or delete on our side. Questions: mjred.dev@gmail.com.

Part 2. SiteProof Team (optional company account)

SiteProof Team is for companies. A company owner turns it on, invites the crew and office staff, and decides which crews see which projects. Nobody needs an account to use SiteProof on their own, and signing in is never required to take photos.

Whose data it is

Data in a SiteProof Team company belongs to that company. The company's owner controls it: who is a member, what each crew sees, what is shared with clients, and what is deleted. We store and process it on the company's behalf, to provide SiteProof Team.

If you are a crew member, an office employee or a client of a company that uses SiteProof Team, questions about the company's projects and photos are best sent to that company first. You can always contact us about your own account at mjred.dev@gmail.com.

Signing in

You sign in with your email address and a 6-digit code that we email to you. There are no passwords. Signing in creates a SiteProof account. We keep:

  • your email address and an internal account ID;
  • the time you signed up and last signed in;
  • the companies you belong to, your role in each (owner, admin, member or viewer), your crews, and the display name you choose.

Other members of your company can see your name, email address and role. The sign-in session is kept on your phone (excluded from Android backups) or in your browser's storage until you sign out. The website uses no cookies and no analytics.

What a company's account stores

When a company uses SiteProof Team, the phones of its members sync the company's projects to the cloud. Owners and admins sync all of the company's projects. Members sync the company projects their crews can see, and projects they create after joining. A member's personal projects from before they joined stay on their phone and are not uploaded.

For those projects we store:

  • Projects, jobs and albums: names, notes, job numbers, job site addresses and site location, dates, and client contact details the company enters (name, phone, email and address).
  • Photos: the photo files in three sizes (full size, a 1600-pixel web copy and a thumbnail), and their details: time and time zone, GPS location (latitude, longitude, accuracy, altitude and direction), street address, caption, notes, tags, markup, rotation, before/after pairs, original file name and size.
    • The full-size file keeps the camera's EXIF data, including the GPS location.
    • Redacted photos: when a photo has a blur or redaction, only a copy with the redaction burned in is uploaded. The unredacted original never leaves the phone. If a redaction is added after upload, the uploaded original is replaced by the redacted copy.
    • Stamped copies are not uploaded; each phone draws its own stamp.
  • Checklists run on a job: items, status, notes and the photos attached.
  • Documents the company uploads for its clients (for example invoices, estimates, permits and contracts, as PDF, JPEG or PNG), with their title, amount, due date, status and the company's own payment link. SiteProof never handles payments between a company and its clients; a payment link opens the company's own payment page.
  • Messages: team notes, each project's team chat, messages with clients, photo comments and their earlier versions when edited, client approvals, "I've sent payment" notices, and which messages each person has read.
    • @mentions: when someone mentions a teammate in a team message, the message stores a reference to that person's account, and we keep a record of the mention and whether it has been read.
  • Notification settings of each member, per company: which emails and phone notifications they want (all, mentions only, or none), sound, email language and muted projects.
  • Phone notification tokens (see “Phone notifications” below).
  • Company branding for the client page: company name, logo, accent colour, phone, email, website, licence number and welcome message.
  • Activity history (see below).
  • Who created and last changed each item, and an ID of the phone (or "web") that made the change.

Activity history

So that a company can see what happened and recover from mistakes, every change to the company's data is recorded: who made it, when, from which device (a random ID of the phone's SiteProof installation, or "web"), and what changed (the old and the new values, for example a renamed job or an edited note). It also records client link opens (once a day), client comments and approvals, documents opened by a client, and payment notices. Anyone who can see a project can see its history.

Why we use this data

Only to provide SiteProof Team: to sign you in, sync the company's projects between phones and the website, show the right projects to the right people, send the emails described below, let the company share work with its clients, keep the history and the Trash, protect the service from abuse, and answer support requests. We do not use it for advertising, we do not sell it, and we do not build profiles of you.

Who can see what

  • Owners and admins see all of the company's projects, its members and crews, the Trash, client links with their open logs, and the activity history. Admins and owners can delete items into the Trash; only the owner can delete permanently.
  • Members see the projects shared with their crews, and the projects they create. They can add and edit, and archive instead of deleting.
  • Viewers can see the projects shared with their crews but not change them.
  • Team chat: each project has a team chat. Every member who can see the project can read it and, unless they are a viewer, write in it; owners and admins see every project's chat. Comments on the project's jobs and photos (team notes, and messages in client conversations) also appear in it as short notices for the team. You can only @mention people who can see the project.
  • Clients see only what the company shares with them (see “The client page” below). They never see the team chat, team-only notes, the activity history, archived or deleted items, or other clients' messages.
  • Us: our staff access company data only when needed to run the service, to fix a problem, to answer your request, or when the law requires it.

The client page (siteproof.plainform.app)

A company can share a project or a job with a client through a private link (siteproof.plainform.app/p/…), and clients can sign in on the company's page with their email address and a 6-digit code.

  • What a client sees: the photos the company chose, with markup and redactions applied, each photo's caption, time and street address, the company's branding, client-visible documents, and the messages in that client's conversation. If the company allows downloads, the client can download a 1600-pixel copy without any location data in the file. If the company also allows originals, the client can download full-size files, which keep the camera's GPS location.
  • Anyone who has the link can see what it shares, including the client conversation. Companies can set links to expire, and can revoke or replace them at any time. To comment, approve or say that a payment was sent, the client must sign in with the email address the company shared with.
  • What we record about visitors: when a link is opened, we store the time, a keyed hash of the visitor's IP address (a scrambled form that is not the address itself), and the first 160 characters of the browser's user-agent string (which names the browser and operating system). The company's owners and admins can see these records (the number of opens and devices). They are deleted after 12 months. The website itself sets no cookies and has no analytics; it keeps a signed-in client's session in the browser's storage until they sign out.
  • What we record about clients who sign in: their email address, the name they give with a comment, their comments, approvals (with the photos shown at that moment) and payment notices, and which messages they have read. The company sees all of this.

Emails

We send these emails, using the email service Resend, from noreply@plainform.app:

  • Sign-in codes, to the address you sign in with.
  • To clients: a new message from the company in their conversation, and a new document or invoice made visible to them, with a link to the client page. The sender name is the company; replies go to the company.
  • To the company (the reply-to or email address in its branding, or else its owners): a client's message, approval or "I've sent payment" notice.
  • To a company's owners: a notice when the company's deletion is scheduled, and a reminder 3 days before it is permanently deleted.
  • To team members, about team chat: an email when someone @mentions you (at most one per conversation every 5 minutes; later mentions are grouped into the next one), and, only if you choose it, a summary of unread messages in a project's team chat (at most one per project every 30 minutes). These come from "SiteProof", not from the company, and include the sender's name, the project name and the message text, with a link to the chat. Nothing is sent for a message you have already read. By default you get mention emails only. You can change this (all, mentions only, or none), choose the email language, or mute a project (mentions still arrive unless you choose none) in the notification settings: in the app under Settings › Company › Notifications, or on the website in the chat's notification settings.

Client and company notification emails are sent at most once per conversation every 10 minutes to each person. We send no marketing email. A copy of each notification email's content is kept with the company's data for 30 days after it is sent (or cancelled, or given up after failed tries), then deleted. Invite codes and client links that you send yourself (for example by email or text message) go through your own apps.

Phone notifications (team chat)

If you are signed in to SiteProof Team on a phone, the app can notify you of new team chat messages and @mentions. It uses Google Firebase Cloud Messaging (FCM), the standard Android service for delivering notifications. We use Firebase only for this: no Firebase Analytics, crash reporting or any other Firebase product.

  • What is stored: when you sign in, the app gets a notification token from Google for that phone and sends it to our server, with the platform, the app's language and the app version. To issue the token, Google's Firebase component on the phone sends Google a Firebase installation ID (a random ID of the app's installation).
  • What Google receives for each notification: the token and a few internal IDs (the kind of notification, the company, the project, the conversation and the message). No message text, names or email addresses. The app then fetches the message from our server over HTTPS and shows the notification itself. On a locked phone it shows only a generic line ("New team message"), unless you allow notification details on the lock screen in Android's settings.
  • When FCM is used: only while you are signed in to SiteProof Team. Without an account, or signed out, the app does not contact Firebase.
  • Your choices: all project-chat messages and mentions (the default), mentions only, or none, and muted projects, in the notification settings (app: Settings › Company › Notifications; website: the chat's notification settings). You can also turn off SiteProof's notifications in Android's settings.
  • Removal: signing out removes the phone's token from our server and deletes it at Google; deleting your account removes all your tokens. Tokens that Google reports as no longer valid are deleted too.

Service providers and where data is stored

We use these companies to run SiteProof Team. They process data for us under their own terms and privacy policies, and only to provide their service:

  • Supabase — Database, sign-in (accounts and codes) and file storage for photos, logos and documents. Where: United States (AWS US East, N. Virginia).
  • Cloudflare — Hosts the website siteproof.plainform.app and its relay: the website's requests to our database pass through Cloudflare, which forwards the visitor's IP address to our server for the link-open records and for rate limiting. The relay does not store what passes through it. Where: Cloudflare's global network.
  • Resend — Sends the emails above. Resend keeps each email it sends, and its delivery log, for 30 days. Where: United States.
  • Google Firebase Cloud Messaging — Delivers phone notifications for team chat (see “Phone notifications” above). Receives the phone's notification token and internal IDs, never message content. Where: Google.
  • Google Play — Payments for SiteProof Pro in the app. Where: Google.
  • Google ML Kit — Optional translation (see its section). Where: Google.
  • Stripe (when billing launches) — Payments for SiteProof Team subscriptions, made by company owners on the SiteProof website. Stripe will handle the card details; we will receive the subscription's status, number of seats and billing contact, never full card details. The app never takes Team payments. Where: Stripe.

Like most online services, our providers keep technical logs for security and troubleshooting:

  • Supabase keeps server logs of requests to the database, sign-in and our server functions, which include IP addresses and times of requests, for up to 7 days. Its sign-in audit log, which records sign-ins and sign-outs with the account, time, IP address and browser, is also kept for up to 7 days.
  • Cloudflare carries the website's requests on its network under its own privacy policy and keeps its network logs only for a limited time. The website's relay does not store logs of what passes through it.
  • Resend keeps the emails it sends and their delivery logs for 30 days.

We do not sell personal data and we do not share it with anyone for their own purposes. We may disclose data if the law requires it, or to a buyer of SiteProof if the business is sold, in which case this policy continues to apply.

How long we keep it

  • Your account is kept until you delete it (see below). If your company is deleted, your account stays, without that company; an account with no company holds only your email address, and you can delete it yourself at any time. Sign-in codes expire after 1 hour.
  • Company data is kept as long as the company uses SiteProof Team, until the company deletes it or its owner deletes the company. A deleted company is switched off at once and its data is permanently deleted 30 days later (see below).
  • Deleted items: when an owner or admin deletes something, it goes to the company's Trash and can be restored. It stays there until the owner deletes it permanently, which is possible after 30 days. Nothing in the Trash is deleted automatically. Permanent deletion removes the item, everything under it, and its photo or document files.
  • Activity history is kept for as long as the company's data is kept. When an item is permanently deleted, its history entries keep who made each change and when, but the old values (such as an earlier name or note) are replaced by a marker saying the item was deleted; the entry for the permanent deletion keeps a short summary, such as the item's name or title. If you need an entry removed, contact us.
  • Notification emails: the copy kept with the company's data is deleted 30 days after the email is sent, cancelled or given up.
  • Phone notifications: a phone's notification token is kept until you sign out on that phone, delete your account, or Google reports it as no longer valid (we keep at most your 20 most recent phones). Our record of each notification (IDs only, no text) is deleted 7 days after it is sent or cancelled.
  • Notification settings and mentions are kept while you are in the company; leaving the company or deleting your account deletes them.
  • Client link opens (time, hashed IP address and browser user agent) are deleted after 12 months.
  • Deletion records: see “Deleting your account and your data” below.
  • Server logs at our providers: up to 7 days at Supabase, 30 days for sent emails at Resend, and a limited time at Cloudflare (see above).
  • Backups: our database provider, Supabase, keeps daily backups of the database for up to 7 days. Deleted data can remain in those backups until they expire. Backups are used only to recover from a failure.
  • On phones: a phone keeps its copy of the company's projects until they are deleted on the phone or the app is uninstalled. Leaving a company or signing out stops syncing, but what is already on that phone stays there.
    • When you delete your account in the app, the company projects are removed from that phone. Work that had not been uploaded yet stays on the phone, in a personal project marked "(kept)". Other phones you used (for example, if you delete your account on the website) keep their copy until it is deleted there or the app is uninstalled.
    • When a company is permanently deleted, its projects are removed from each member's phone, in the same way, the next time the app connects to SiteProof Team. A phone that does not connect again keeps its copy until it is deleted there or the app is uninstalled.

Deleting your account and your data

SiteProof by MJRed Dev LLC. You can delete your SiteProof Team account yourself at any time, and a company's owner can delete the whole company:

  1. In the app: Settings › Company › Delete account, or Delete company (owners).
  2. On the web: siteproof.plainform.app/account/delete. Sign in with your email address and a 6-digit code, then choose what to delete. This works without the app, and it is also how a client who signs in on a company's client page deletes their account.

To protect you, you confirm with a fresh 6-digit code that we email to you. Deleting your account then happens at once and cannot be undone. To delete a company, you also type its exact name; a company is switched off at once and permanently deleted after 30 days (see below). If you cannot use either way, email mjred.dev@gmail.com from the address you sign in with.

When you delete your account, we delete at once:

  • your sign-in account (your email address), your memberships, roles, crews and display name, and your read markers;
  • your permission to create a company, and every notification email addressed to you, sent or waiting;
  • your notification settings, your phones' notification tokens and pending phone notifications, and the records of mentions of you;
  • your client sign-in: links a company shared with you stay the company's record, but are no longer tied to your email address.

What stays with the company, and why: photos, notes, messages, approvals, payment notices and other work you added to a company's projects belong to that company, so they stay with it, but no longer show who you are:

  • on your messages, approvals and payment notices, your name and email address are removed, and they show "Former member" (or "Former client" for a client); where someone @mentioned you in a message, the mention shows "Former member";
  • your email address is removed from notification emails waiting to be sent and from the activity history text;
  • history entries of changes you made keep an internal account ID, which no longer links to any person once your account is deleted.

If you want company data itself removed, ask the company's owner, or contact us and we will pass your request on.

Owners: a company must have an owner. If you are a company's only owner, first make someone else owner, or delete the company.

When an owner deletes a company, the company is switched off at once: team access, syncing and the client page stop. Its data and files are kept for 30 days, during which any of its owners can restore it. We email the owners when the deletion is scheduled, and again 3 days before it happens. After 30 days we permanently delete all of the company's data and files: its projects, jobs, photos and their files, checklists, documents, messages, approvals, payment notices, activity history, Trash, client links and their open records, members, invites and branding (logo included). The sign-in accounts of its owners and members are not deleted: they are only removed from the company. An account that belongs to no company holds only its email address, and each person can delete their own account at any time, as above.

What we keep after a deletion: a deletion record of each request, holding only a one-way hash of the account ID (a scrambled form, not the ID itself), the action, the time, the outcome, the number of items deleted and, for a company, its random internal ID. It holds no name, email address or content, and lets us show that a request was carried out. Apart from it, deleted data can remain only in our providers' server logs, sent-email logs and database backups until they expire (see “How long we keep it”), and in copies already on phones or downloaded by people the company shared with.

Without an account: nothing to delete on our side (see Part 1).

Security

  • SiteProof Team data travels encrypted (HTTPS) between the app, the website and our servers.
  • The database enforces who can see what (row-level security): every request is checked against the company, the crew and the role of the person making it.
  • Photo, logo and document files are in private storage. They are reached only through links that expire after minutes (signed links); there are no public file addresses.
  • Client links are long random codes. We store only a hash of each link, so even we cannot read a link back; a company can revoke or replace a link at any time.
  • The website talks only to its own address. Its relay forwards only the kinds of requests SiteProof needs, and drops cookies and other browser details that the server does not need.
  • Members cannot delete projects, jobs, albums, photos or documents; they archive them instead. Deleted items stay restorable in the Trash for at least 30 days.
  • The app and the website hold only a public key. The keys that bypass these rules stay on our servers.

No system is perfectly secure. If we learn of a breach that affects your data, we will tell the affected companies and people as the law requires.

For everyone

Permissions and why

  • Camera — Taking photos.
  • Location, only while the app is in use — Stamping each photo with where it was taken, and suggesting the job you are at. SiteProof never uses location in the background. With SiteProof Team, a company photo's location is synced with the photo.
  • Microphone (optional) — Voice notes and voice commands. Speech is recognised on the phone by Android's on-device recognizer. Audio is never recorded to a file, kept, or sent anywhere.
  • Notifications (optional) — Progress while a report is being made, the status of "Share to computer", and sync; with SiteProof Team, team chat messages and @mentions (see “Phone notifications”).
  • Network (internet, Wi-Fi state) — Looking up street addresses; Google Play purchases; "Share to computer" on your local Wi-Fi; SiteProof Team sign-in, sync and team chat notifications (through Firebase Cloud Messaging), if you use it; downloading translation language packs, and ML Kit's diagnostics, if you use translation.

Services SiteProof relies on

  • Address lookup. To turn a photo's GPS coordinates into a street address, SiteProof uses Android's built-in geocoding service (android.location.Geocoder). On most phones Google provides this service, and it receives the coordinates to answer the lookup. SiteProof does not send them to any server of ours for this, and the lookup includes no photo or other information. Addresses are cached on the phone so the same place is not looked up twice. If location is off, no lookup happens.
  • Google Play Billing. If you buy SiteProof Pro, Google Play processes the payment under Google's own privacy policy. SiteProof receives only a record that the purchase exists, which it uses to unlock Pro. It never sees your payment details.

Optional translation (Pro): Google ML Kit

SiteProof Pro can translate names, notes and checklist text between English and Spanish. This feature is off until you use it. Apart from SiteProof Team (including its phone notifications through Firebase Cloud Messaging), which you choose to use, it is the only part of SiteProof that sends anything off the phone on its own.

  • How it works. Translation uses Google's ML Kit on-device translation. It needs a language pack for each language (English and Spanish, about 30 MB each), which the app downloads from Google when you ask for it (by default over Wi-Fi only). After that, translation runs on your phone. The text you translate is not sent to Google or to anyone else. Google states that ML Kit translations "don't require you to send users' text to a remote server".
  • Nothing starts until you use it. SiteProof starts ML Kit only the first time you use translation. If you never translate anything, ML Kit sends nothing.
  • What ML Kit sends to Google once you use it. Google's ML Kit data disclosure lists this data, which ML Kit sends to Google:
    • device information: manufacturer, model, OS version and build, and available ML hardware accelerators;
    • app information: package name and app version;
    • per-installation identifiers, which Google says are "not intended to uniquely identify a user or physical device";
    • performance metrics, such as latency;
    • API configuration;
    • feature data: input and output size, feature version, event type and error codes;
    • for translation, the configured source and target languages; for language identification, the languages identified.
    • Google also says the translation library uses Firebase remote config and Firebase installations for remote configuration and diagnostics. (This is separate from the Firebase Cloud Messaging that SiteProof Team uses for phone notifications; SiteProof uses no other Firebase product.)
  • Why. Google states the data is "used for diagnostics and usage analytics".
  • Protection and sharing. Google states that ML Kit encrypts this data in transit using HTTPS and does not transfer it to third parties. Google handles it under the Google Privacy Policy.
  • Your choice. ML Kit's reporting cannot be switched off while you use translation. To avoid it, do not use translation. You can delete the downloaded language packs in Settings › Translation. SiteProof never receives this data. See Google's own page: ML Kit data disclosure.
  • Without Google Play services translation is unavailable. There is no other translation service.
  • Translations are shown, never saved over your text. Your original notes and names are never changed. In a PDF report, a translated note keeps the original underneath, marked "Translated by Google".
  • Accuracy. Machine translation can be wrong; check anything important against the original. Google's required disclaimer: THIS SERVICE MAY CONTAIN TRANSLATIONS POWERED BY GOOGLE. GOOGLE DISCLAIMS ALL WARRANTIES RELATED TO THE TRANSLATIONS, EXPRESS OR IMPLIED, INCLUDING ANY WARRANTIES OF ACCURACY, RELIABILITY, AND ANY IMPLIED WARRANTIES OF MERCHANTABILITY, FITNESS FOR A PARTICULAR PURPOSE AND NONINFRINGEMENT.
  • Data held by Google. Data that ML Kit sends to Google is held by Google under the Google Privacy Policy, linked only to a per-installation identifier. We cannot delete it. Uninstalling SiteProof or clearing its storage removes that identifier from the phone.

Google's statements above were checked against its ML Kit data disclosure page (last modified 14 May 2025), the ML Kit translation overview and the Cloud Translation attribution page on 28 September 2026.
