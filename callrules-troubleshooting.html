CallRules troubleshooting

Fixes for common problems, and what each card on the app's Troubleshoot screen means.

Start in the app: Settings › Troubleshoot issues

CallRules checks your setup and identifies issues that might prevent blocking from working correctly. Where a card offers Fix, tap it. The refresh icon runs the checks again.

Common problems

  • A call wasn't blocked
  • A contact or allowed number still rings
  • A rule seems to be ignored
  • A contact or group rule matches nobody
  • Outgoing rules don't run
  • An outgoing confirmation was cancelled, expired, or didn't redial
  • A location rule doesn't match
  • No blocked-call notifications
  • Voice commands don't work
  • Lifetime Pro is unavailable or won't restore
  • Still need help?

A call wasn't blocked

  • Check that CallRules is your Caller ID & spam app and that Home shows “Protection Active”.
  • Check that Settings › Call protection and Rules enabled are on.
  • Check that the rule is switched on, covers Incoming calls, hasn't expired, and is inside its schedule and at its location.
  • Open Rules › Check rules. “Block rule is bypassed” means the whitelist, your contacts, or an allow rule lets the call through first.
  • For a call your Phone app marked as spam: CallRules can't read that verdict. Turn on Settings › Block carrier spam labels to block calls your carrier labels as spam, such as “Scam Likely”. Not every carrier sends a label.
  • If CallRules can't decide within about 3.5 seconds, the call rings rather than risk blocking it wrongly.

A contact or allowed number still rings

  • The whitelist, your contacts (under Settings › Contact whitelist), and allow rules are checked before ordinary block rules and blocked numbers.
  • To block such a caller anyway, edit the block rule and turn on “Override allow rules”, or remove the number from the whitelist.
  • A contact, group, or caller-name block rule follows the same order, so it needs “Override allow rules” to block a contact the Contact whitelist lets through.

A rule seems to be ignored

  • Temporary rules show “Expired” on Rules when their time runs out; expired rules are deleted about a day later.
  • Rules with a schedule apply only between Start and End on their active days, in the rule's timezone.
  • Rules with a location apply only when CallRules can tell you're inside the area, from a recent location or Android's geofence for that place. See “Location rules” below.
  • Outgoing rules need Outgoing call protection. Silence, location, and private or unknown callers apply to incoming calls only.
  • If Lifetime Pro isn't active, schedules, locations, and patterns already saved on rules stay but can't be changed.

A contact or group rule matches nobody

  • Contact rules need contacts access. CallRules asks for it when you choose a contact or group.
  • If the contact or group was deleted, the rule's Edit screen shows “Contact not found” and Rule check warns “Contact not found” or “Contact group not found”. Edit the rule or delete it.
  • Caller-name rules match your contact's saved name, or the caller-ID name your carrier sends. Not every carrier sends names.

Outgoing rules don't run

  • Outgoing protection needs Lifetime Pro.
  • Open Settings › Outgoing calls › Outgoing call setup, tap “Allow call redirection”, and turn on “Enable outgoing rules”.
  • Check that the rule's Call direction is Outgoing or Both.
  • If a rule or setting requires a PIN, set the shared PIN first.
  • For approved calls, tap “Allow Phone permission”. CallRules doesn't need Call Log or older outgoing-call permissions.
  • Allow notifications, so a waiting confirmation still shows when Android can't open CallRules directly.
  • Android never sends emergency calls, and some gateway calls, to CallRules.

An outgoing confirmation was cancelled, expired, or didn't redial

  • CallRules cancels the original call before asking. Tapping Cancel call or waiting 60 seconds places no call.
  • After the correct PIN or a completed hold, CallRules checks your rules, the role, and the Phone permission again, then places one replacement call.
  • Each approval works once. If something changed or Android couldn't place the call, fix the reported issue and dial again.

A location rule doesn't match

  • Location access is needed only for enabled incoming rules that have a location.
  • Allow location first, then choose Android's “Allow all the time” option if the rule must work while CallRules is closed or the phone is locked.
  • If CallRules can't tell where you are (no access, no recent location, and no geofence result), Settings › Rules & places › “When location can't be checked” decides. By default an allow rule lets the call ring and a block rule is skipped. Call history notes when this happens.
  • Geofences need precise location and work best when Android doesn't restrict CallRules' battery use. The Location signal check shows both.
  • Location rules match where your phone is, not where the caller is. Area-code, country, and city rules match number prefixes.

No blocked-call notifications

  • Turn on Settings › Show blocked call alerts.
  • Allow Android's notification permission for CallRules. The Notifications check offers Fix.
  • Blocked-call alerts are low priority, so some phones group or hide them. Every blocked call is still listed in Call history.

Voice commands don't work

  • Voice commands need Lifetime Pro.
  • Speech uses only Android's on-device recognizer. If your phone doesn't have one, type in “Type a command” instead.
  • If the card asks “Did you mean …?”, CallRules changed or left out a word. Tap Confirm only if the card shows what you meant.

Lifetime Pro is unavailable or won't restore

  • Use CallRules installed from Google Play and the same Google account that bought Pro.
  • Check your connection, then open Upgrade and tap “Retry Google Play” or “Restore purchase”.
  • Lifetime Pro can be unavailable for some accounts or regions, and in test builds that aren't published.
  • A pending purchase unlocks Pro only after Google Play completes it.

Still need help?

  • Open Settings › Report a problem. Type or dictate what happened, then tap Preview and send it with an email or sharing app.
  • You can attach diagnostics. The log leaves out phone numbers, contacts, rules, coordinates, speech, and purchase tokens.

What the Troubleshoot screen checks

The screen shows only the cards that apply to your setup. Each result below is the exact text the card shows.

Call screening role

Checks: Whether CallRules is your Caller ID & spam app. Android sends calls only to that app for screening.

Shown: Always

  • OKCallRules is set as your caller ID & spam app
  • ProblemCallRules is NOT set as your caller ID & spam app — calls won't be blockedFix: Asks Android to make CallRules your Caller ID & spam app.

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Android Settings › Apps › Default apps › Caller ID & spam app and choose CallRules.
  • Some phones call this “Call screening app” or keep it under Phone settings.

Call protection

Checks: Whether the shield switch is on.

Shown: Always

  • OKProtection is enabled
  • ProblemProtection is disabled — no calls are being blockedFix: Turns Call protection on.

How to fix it yourself

  • Tap the shield on Home, or turn on Settings › Call protection.

Carrier verification

Checks: The Carrier verification setting, which uses the STIR/SHAKEN result your carrier attaches to each call.

Shown: Always

  • WarningTurned off — calls that fail carrier verification are not blockedFix: Sets Carrier verification to Standard.
  • OKStrict — unverified callers are blocked as well as failed ones
  • OKStandard — calls failing carrier verification are blocked

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Settings › Protection › Carrier verification and choose Standard or Strict.
  • Strict also blocks calls with no verification, which can include real calls from small carriers, VoIP services, and some international numbers.

Call Rules

Checks: Whether Rules enabled is on. When it's off, no incoming block or allow rule applies.

Shown: Always

  • OKRules are active
  • WarningAll rules are PAUSED — block, allow, and silence rules are not being appliedFix: Turns Rules enabled back on.

How to fix it yourself

  • Turn on Settings › Rules enabled.

Notifications

Checks: Whether Android lets CallRules show notifications (Android 13 and later ask for this permission).

Shown: Always

  • OKNotification permission granted
  • WarningNotification permission not granted — you won't see blocked call alertsFix: Opens Android's notification settings for CallRules.

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Android Settings › Apps › CallRules › Notifications and allow them.
  • Then check that Settings › Show blocked call alerts is on in CallRules.

Battery usage

Checks: Nothing to fix: this card explains how CallRules runs.

Shown: Always

  • OKCallRules uses Android's event-driven CallScreeningService — it runs when a call arrives, not continuously. Only while you have location rules, it also uses Android's low-power geofences around their places. Battery impact stays very low.

How to fix it yourself

  • No battery optimization changes are needed.

Outgoing Call Redirection role

Checks: Whether CallRules holds Android's Call Redirection role, which lets it check calls you place.

Shown: Only when outgoing protection is set up: outgoing rules are on, a PIN or long press is required for outgoing calls, or an enabled rule covers outgoing calls.

  • OKCallRules can evaluate outgoing calls
  • ProblemRole not granted — outgoing rules cannot runFix: Asks Android to give CallRules the Call Redirection role.
  • ProblemCall redirection is unavailable on this device

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Settings › Outgoing calls › Outgoing call setup and tap “Allow call redirection”.
  • If your phone doesn't offer call redirection, outgoing protection can't run on it.

Outgoing-rule setting

Checks: Whether “Enable outgoing rules” is on when you have enabled Outgoing or Both rules.

Shown: Only when outgoing protection is set up: outgoing rules are on, a PIN or long press is required for outgoing calls, or an enabled rule covers outgoing calls.

  • OKOutgoing and Both rules are enabled
  • OKNo enabled Outgoing or Both rules; global confirmation can still apply
  • WarningOutgoing or Both rules exist, but outgoing rule evaluation is turned offFix: Opens Outgoing call protection.

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Settings › Outgoing calls › Outgoing call setup and turn on “Enable outgoing rules”.

Outgoing PIN

Checks: Whether the shared PIN is set when outgoing calls need one.

Shown: Only when “Require PIN for all outgoing calls” is on, or an enabled outgoing rule has “Require PIN”, and no PIN is set.

  • ProblemA PIN-required outgoing call will fail closed until a shared PIN is setFix: Opens Outgoing call protection.

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Settings › Outgoing calls › Outgoing call setup and tap Set PIN (4 to 8 digits).
  • Rules imported with “Require PIN” need a PIN set on this phone.

Confirmed-call redial

Checks: Whether CallRules has the Phone permission it needs to place the approved call again.

Shown: Only when outgoing calls need a PIN or a long press.

  • OKPhone permission is ready for an approved replacement call
  • ProblemPhone permission is missing — approval cannot place the replacement callFix: Asks for the Phone permission.

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Settings › Outgoing calls › Outgoing call setup and tap “Allow Phone permission”.
  • CallRules doesn't need Call Log or older outgoing-call permissions.

Confirmation timeout and cancellation

Checks: Nothing to fix: this card explains what happens to a call waiting for approval.

Shown: Only when outgoing calls need a PIN or a long press.

  • OKA cancelled or expired request places no call. If approved redial fails, verify the role and Phone permission, then dial again.

How to fix it yourself

  • If you tap Cancel call or wait 60 seconds, no call is placed. Dial again.
  • Each approval places one call. If the redial fails, check the role and Phone permission, then dial again.

Location rules

Checks: Whether CallRules may use your location, including while it's closed or the phone is locked.

Shown: Only when an enabled incoming rule has a location.

  • OKReady while CallRules is closed or the phone is locked
  • WarningLocation is set to Approximate. Location rules need Precise location to tell when you're at a place.Fix: Opens Android's settings for CallRules, where you can allow precise location and change battery use.
  • WarningForeground access is allowed; background access is still needed for calls while the app is closedFix: Asks for location access, then explains how to allow it all the time.
  • WarningLocation access is unavailable; location rules follow your “When location can't be checked” settingFix: Asks for location access, then explains how to allow it all the time.

How to fix it yourself

  • Open Settings › Location rules and tap “Allow location for location rules”.
  • Then tap “Set up background location” and choose Android's “Allow all the time” option.
  • To allow precise location, open Android Settings › Apps › CallRules › Permissions › Location and turn on “Use precise location”.
  • Without location access, location rules can't be checked, and Settings › Rules & places › “When location can't be checked” decides what they do.

Location signal

Checks: Whether location rules can be checked when a call arrives: background battery use and the age of your last known location.

Shown: Only when an enabled incoming rule has a location and precise location is allowed.

  • OKBattery use isn't restricted, and a recent location is available
  • WarningAndroid restricts CallRules' background battery use — geofence updates can be late or missedFix: Opens Android's settings for CallRules, where you can allow precise location and change battery use.
  • WarningYour last known location is more than 10 minutes old — location rules rely on geofences until a new one arrives. That's normal when no app has used location lately.

How to fix it yourself

  • Location rules use a location from the last 10 minutes, or else Android's geofences around their places. Geofences need precise location.
  • If Android restricts CallRules' battery use, open Android Settings › Apps › CallRules › Battery and choose Unrestricted.
  • When neither can tell, Settings › Rules & places › “When location can't be checked” decides what the rule does.

Google Play Billing

Checks: Whether CallRules can reach Google Play Billing and load Lifetime Pro.

Shown: Always

  • WarningCould not connect or load the lifetime product. Check Play Store connectivity, then retry.Fix: Reconnects to Google Play Billing.
  • WarningConnected, but the lifetime product is not available for this account or build.Fix: Reconnects to Google Play Billing.
  • OKBilling connection and purchase restoration are available from Upgrade.

How to fix it yourself

  • Check your connection and that the Google Play Store app is signed in and up to date.
  • Open Upgrade and tap “Retry Google Play” or “Restore purchase”.
  • See “Lifetime Pro is unavailable or won't restore” under Common problems.
